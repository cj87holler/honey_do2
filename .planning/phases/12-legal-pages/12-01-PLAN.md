<task type="auto">
  <name>Task 1: Shared legal constants and the Privacy Policy page</name>
  <files>src/lib/legal.ts, src/app/privacy/page.tsx</files>
  <action>
Create `src/lib/legal.ts` exporting `LEGAL_CONTACT_EMAIL = "honeydoapp@gmail.com"` and a
`LEGAL_LAST_UPDATED` date string. Both pages import from here so the address lives in exactly one
place — it is expected to change once a custom domain exists.

Create `src/app/privacy/page.tsx` as a plain top-level route (NOT inside `(app)`, which would
apply the authenticated header). Export `metadata` with a title. Match the landing page's
bee-theme styling — amber/stone palette, same container widths — but keep the tone plain. Legal
pages are where the playfulness yields to clarity.

Content must be grounded in `src/db/schema.ts`, not boilerplate. It must state:

- **Account data**: name, email, and a hashed password (hashed by Better Auth, never stored in
  plain text).
- **Session data**: explicitly including **IP address and user agent**, which the `session` table
  stores on every row.
- **Hive and task data**: hive names, honey counts, join dates, and **free-form task text** — up
  to 160 characters that users may fill with anything, so the policy must not claim the app holds
  only structured data.
- **Invite data**: invite tokens and who created/redeemed them.
- **Administrator access**: holders of the configured admin addresses can view all users and all
  hives, and can reset any user's password. This is a real access path to other people's data and
  must be disclosed rather than buried.
- **Sub-processors**: Vercel (hosting) and Neon (database), named accurately. Error tracking is
  described in forward-looking generic terms only — do NOT claim Sentry is integrated, because it
  is not until Phase 16.
- **Retention and deletion**: there is no in-app delete-account feature. State plainly that
  deletion is requested by emailing the contact address and is performed manually. Do not imply a
  self-serve mechanism exists.
- **Contact**: the shared constant.

Do not invent compliance claims. No GDPR/CCPA rights language, no cookie-consent claims, no
"we never share your data with third parties" absolutes that the sub-processor list contradicts.
  </action>
  <verify>npm run typecheck passes; visiting /privacy renders without auth</verify>
  <done>Privacy page exists at a public route with schema-grounded content</done>
</task>

  <files>src/app/terms/page.tsx</files>
  <action>
Create `src/app/terms/page.tsx`, same public-route and styling treatment as `/privacy`.

Content: acceptance of terms, a description of the service (a free, gamified household task app),
account responsibilities (accurate email, keep your password secure, you are responsible for
activity under your account), acceptable use (no illegal or abusive content in the free-form task
text — this ties directly to the fact that task text is unmoderated user input), a statement that
the service is provided as-is with no warranty and no uptime guarantee, that accounts may be
terminated for abuse, that the terms may change with the "last updated" date as the signal, and
the contact address.

**No governing-law or dispute-resolution clause** — locked decision in CONTEXT.md. Honey_Do is a
free app with no payments; naming the wrong jurisdiction is worse than naming none.
